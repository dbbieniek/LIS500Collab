Implicit Bias Resource Guide

Implicit Bias is an unconscious belief about a social group that influences how one perceives and behaves toward others. People may not be aware that they hold them or recognize how they affect their actions. Implicit bias can influence decisions and interactions in areas like education, employment, healthcare, and technology, leading to unfair treatment or discrimination. Recognizing these biases is an important step toward understanding how they affect judgments and working to reduce their influence.

The Implicit Bias Test is a psychological assessment that measures implicit biases towards different social groups. It is designed to reveal automatic associations between concepts, such as social groups and positive or negative characteristics, that a person may not consciously recognize. The test typically measures how quickly someone categorizes words or images, using differences in reaction times to identify potential biases.

Intersectionality is the idea that one's experiences are shaped by several different factors, such as race, gender, class, and sexuality. These factors can overlap and interact with one another, creating unique experiences of privilege, discrimination, or inequality. A person's experiences may differ depending on how their race and gender intersect, rather than being shaped by either factor alone. Intersectionality helps explain why individuals within the same social groups may face different challenges.
